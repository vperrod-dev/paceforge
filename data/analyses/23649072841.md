Structurally, though, the fartlek didn't happen. The fastest moment in the
entire time-series is a brief ~4:24/km (264 sec/km) just after the mid-run

progression per half. The anaerobic training effect of 1.3 confirms it: the
"speed play" never got fast. What you actually ran was a progressive
moderate run whose intensity came from heat and hills rather than deliberate
surges — the last ~10 minutes sat at or above your lactate threshold HR of
163 bpm, and the watch logged 12:01 in HR zone 5 (153+) against only 5:49 in
zone 2.

## Effect on your profile

This landed in **week 4, the cutback week** ("absorbing the last three weeks,
not adding more load"). Volume-wise it behaved — 6.85 km on a 14.6 km/week
base is exactly the right size. Intensity-wise it overshot the recovery
intent from the other direction than planned: instead of six short, crisp
surges with real recoveries, you accumulated a long block above threshold
(avg HR 160–165 for the final 15 minutes, peaking at 91% of your 195 max).
Aerobic TE 3.3 is a solid stimulus and VO2max held at 53, and with training
readiness at 66 and HRV balanced you absorbed it fine — but a heat-and-hills
threshold grind is a different (and more fatiguing) session than the
neuromuscular sharpness the fartlek was there to maintain. That sharpness
stimulus was simply skipped this week.

## What to improve

- **Make surges discrete and honest.** A fartlek only does its job if the
  hard bits are genuinely hard and brief: next time, 2:00 at ~3:55/km feels
  like a controlled 5K-race surge, then genuinely jog (let HR fall back
  toward ~130) before the next one. If terrain is hilly, surge on the flats
  or use "hard up / float down" — but keep the on/off contrast. Anaerobic TE
  1.3 vs aerobic 3.3 is the tell that everything blurred into one effort.
- **Cap heat-and-hill efforts by HR, not pace.** At 24°C and 83% humidity,
  4:54/km up 17m/km of gain cost you 160–178 bpm — deep into zone 5 on what
  a cutback week wanted as controlled work. On holiday terrain in heat,
  treat ~163 bpm (your LT) as the ceiling for anything that isn't a planned
  surge, and let pace drift where it must.
- **Cadence is still the economy lever.** 160 spm average with a 1.25m
  stride and 269ms ground contact is the same over-striding signature as
  your recent easy runs — turnover barely moved even when pace dropped to
  4:24/km. Vertical ratio (7.88%) is efficient, so mechanics are sound;
  just cue quicker feet, aiming ~166–170 spm on the next easy run and on
  every surge.
