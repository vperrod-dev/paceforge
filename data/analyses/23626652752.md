Holiday long run in Salou on 2026-07-17, 7.12 km in 38:22 (avg pace 5:23/km,
avg HR 135 bpm, max 162) with 78m of elevation gain in warm, humid conditions
(23°C, 89% humidity). The shape was a textbook easy progression: the first

before the fastest full kilometre closed the run at 5:08/km with HR back down
to 142. Training effect: aerobic 3.1 (productive), anaerobic 0.0. Cadence
averaged 159 spm, stride length 1.17m, ground contact 279ms, vertical ratio
8.22%.

## Versus the plan

(4:35–5:03/km) — and compliance flags it **green**: 7.12 km vs 7.0 planned
(ratio 1.02). On raw pace you were outside the window: 5:23/km average,
20 s/km slower than the slow edge of the band. But the band doesn't know
about 89% humidity or 78m of Salou hills, and your heart rate does: 135 bpm
average is 69% of your 195 max and nearly 30 beats under your 163 lactate
threshold. That is exactly what "easy" is supposed to cost. The anaerobic
training effect of 0.0 confirms nothing crept in — this was the pure aerobic
session the plan asked for, executed by effort instead of by the watch face.
The only leak: the two big climbs pushed you into zone 4/5 for a combined
~7 minutes (4:08 in Z4, 2:54 in Z5, max 162 on the km-6 hill), against 30+
minutes in Z2/Z3.

Week 3's focus is aerobic endurance + strength (hills), and this run served
both: 38 minutes of time-on-feet with an aerobic TE of 3.1 — a productive
endurance stimulus with essentially zero anaerobic cost, cheap to recover
from. VO2max held at 53, training readiness sat at 66 with HRV balanced, so
this load was absorbed without dents. Just as valuable is the pacing
discipline signal: your recent easy runs have tended to drift toward
moderate effort, and here you held HR 135 for the duration and still
negative-split (5:41/km average over the first 2 km → 5:08 for the last full

intensity-discipline run of the block.

## What to improve

- **Cadence is the standing item.** 159 spm average with a 1.17m stride and
  279ms ground contact is the same low-turnover signature as your other easy
  runs — and it barely moved between 5:40/km and 5:08/km (155 → 161 spm).
  On the next easy run, cue quicker, quieter feet for 1-minute blocks aiming
  ~166–170 spm; shorter ground contact is free economy at every pace.
- **Cap the hills, keep the day easy.** The km-6 climb took HR to 162 — a
  beat under threshold — and the run banked ~7 minutes of Z4/Z5 purely from
  two ascents. On easy days, shorten the stride and let the pace balloon
  uphill so HR stays under ~150; save threshold beats for sessions that ask
  for them.
- **Keep trusting effort over the pace window in heat.** 5:23/km at HR 135
  in these conditions was the right call, not a miss. When conditions are
  this humid, treat the plan's pace band as a fair-weather reference and run
  easy days to HR — exactly as you did here.
